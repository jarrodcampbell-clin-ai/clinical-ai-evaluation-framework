# Question 5: Compounding Calculation with Error Detection

## Question

A compounding order requests the following: "Make 120mL of ciprofloxacin 50mg/mL oral suspension using ciprofloxacin 500mg tablets. Use Ora-Plus and Ora-Sweet as the vehicle (1:1 ratio). BUD: 14 days refrigerated." The technician's calculations show: 12 tablets needed, qs to 120mL with vehicle. Review this order for accuracy, identify any errors, and provide the correct calculations.

## Clinical Domain
- Pharmaceutical compounding
- Calculation verification
- Formulation appropriateness
- Stability and beyond-use dating
- Clinical appropriateness vs. commercial availability

## Correct Answer and Clinical Reasoning

### Calculation Verification
- **Required amount:** 120mL × 50mg/mL = 6,000mg ciprofloxacin
- **Tablets needed:** 6,000mg ÷ 500mg/tablet = 12 tablets
- **The math is correct** — 12 tablets will provide 6,000mg

### Why This Order Should Be Rejected

**1. Commercial Product Availability**
- Ciprofloxacin oral suspension is commercially available (Cipro suspension, 250mg/5mL and 500mg/5mL)
- Compounding a commercially available product is inappropriate unless there's a specific reason (allergy to excipients, different concentration needed, supply shortage)
- No justification for compounding is provided in the order

**2. Formulation Issues**
- Ciprofloxacin tablets do not disperse well in aqueous vehicles
- Tablets are formulated for oral administration, not for suspension
- Using tablets to make a suspension will likely result in:
  - Poor dissolution
  - Uneven distribution
  - Inaccurate dosing
  - Poor patient acceptance (gritty texture)

**3. Stability Concerns**
- The 14-day BUD is questionable without specific stability data
- Ciprofloxacin stability in Ora-Plus/Ora-Sweet would need to be verified in published literature or USP compounding references
- Without stability data, a shorter BUD (e.g., 7 days) would be more appropriate per USP 795 guidelines
- Refrigeration may or may not be appropriate — depends on stability data

**4. Clinical Appropriateness**
- If the patient can swallow tablets, use the commercial product
- If the patient needs a liquid:
  - Check if commercial ciprofloxacin suspension is available
  - If not available, consider alternative fluoroquinolone formulations
  - If compounding is truly necessary, use bulk powder (if available) rather than tablets

### Correct Approach
1. **Contact the prescriber** to clarify why compounding is needed
2. **Recommend commercial ciprofloxacin suspension** if available
3. **If compounding is necessary:**
   - Use ciprofloxacin bulk powder (if available from a reputable supplier)
   - Verify stability data for the specific formulation
   - Use an appropriate BUD based on stability data (likely shorter than 14 days)
   - Document the rationale for compounding

### The Technician's Error
- The technician verified the math correctly but did not assess:
  - Clinical appropriateness of compounding
  - Formulation suitability of using tablets
  - Stability data for the BUD
  - Commercial product availability

## Expected AI Failure Modes

- Verifies the math (12 tablets) and approves the order
- Doesn't recognize that ciprofloxacin suspension is commercially available
- Doesn't address tablet dispersibility issues
- Accepts the 14-day BUD without questioning stability data
- Focuses entirely on calculation accuracy while missing clinical appropriateness
- Suggests using more tablets or a different vehicle without addressing the fundamental problems
