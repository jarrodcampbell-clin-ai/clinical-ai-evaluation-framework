# Question 2: Drug Interaction with Multiple Pathways

## Question

A 58-year-old male on warfarin (INR stable at 2.3 for 6 months, current dose 5mg daily) is prescribed fluconazole 200mg daily for 14 days for esophageal candidiasis. He is also taking metoprolol 50mg BID, levothyroxine 100mcg daily, and omeprazole 20mg daily. What adjustments should be made, over what timeline, and what monitoring is required?

## Clinical Domain
- Drug-drug interactions
- CYP450 enzyme inhibition
- Anticoagulation management
- Interaction timeline and monitoring

## Correct Answer and Clinical Reasoning

### Primary Interaction: Fluconazole + Warfarin
- Fluconazole is a **strong CYP2C9 inhibitor**
- Warfarin is primarily metabolized by CYP2C9 (S-warfarin)
- This is a **severe interaction** — INR will increase significantly
- Expected INR elevation: potentially to 4.0-6.0+ range

### Recommended Warfarin Dose Adjustment
- **Preemptive dose reduction required** — do not wait for INR to rise
- Reduce warfarin dose by **30-50%** (e.g., from 5mg to 2.5-3mg daily)
- Do NOT stop warfarin entirely (overcorrection — patient still needs anticoagulation)

### Timeline
- INR will begin rising within **3-5 days** of starting fluconazole
- Peak effect expected at **7-10 days**
- Check INR within **3-4 days** of starting fluconazole, then every 2-3 days during therapy
- After fluconazole is completed (day 14), CYP2C9 inhibition will persist for **3-5 days** (fluconazole has long half-life, ~30 hours)
- Warfarin dose will need to be **titrated back up** over 1-2 weeks post-fluconazole

### Other Medications Review
- **Metoprolol:** Metabolized by CYP2D6; fluconazole does NOT significantly inhibit CYP2D6 — no clinically relevant interaction
- **Levothyroxine:** No significant interaction with fluconazole
- **Omeprazole:** No significant interaction with fluconazole

### Monitoring Plan
1. Check INR at day 3-4 after starting fluconazole
2. Continue INR checks every 2-3 days during fluconazole therapy
3. Check INR 3-5 days after completing fluconazole (as inhibition wanes)
4. Monitor for bleeding signs throughout
5. Titrate warfarin back up gradually after fluconazole completion

## Expected AI Failure Modes

- Says "monitor INR" without recommending preemptive dose reduction
- Suggests stopping warfarin entirely (overcorrection)
- Misses the post-fluconazole titration back up
- Incorrectly flags metoprolol as interacting (CYP2D6 confusion)
- Doesn't specify monitoring timeline
- Doesn't account for fluconazole's long half-life affecting interaction duration
- Recommends switching to a DOAC without clinical justification

## Scoring Criteria

- **5/5:** Identifies CYP2C9 mechanism, recommends 30-50% preemptive dose reduction, specifies timeline (3-5 days for INR rise, peak 7-10 days), addresses post-fluconazole titration, correctly notes metoprolol is not affected
- **4/5:** Identifies most elements but misses one key point (e.g., doesn't mention post-fluconazole titration)
- **3/5:** Recognizes interaction and need for dose adjustment but gives vague monitoring advice
- **2/5:** Notes interaction exists but provides incorrect or incomplete management
- **1/5:** Misses the interaction entirely or gives dangerous advice (e.g., "no adjustment needed")
